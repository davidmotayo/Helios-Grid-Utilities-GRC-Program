**HELIOS GRID UTILITIES — IT/OT CYBERSECURITY GOVERNANCE PROGRAM**
Executive Summary | Prepared for the Board Risk Committee | 30 July 2026

Helios Grid Utilities is a regional electricity distribution operator supplying roughly 1.4 million households and businesses across Frankfurt and the surrounding districts. As a designated critical-infrastructure operator under Germany's KRITIS regime (Federal Office for Information Security critical-infrastructure regulation) and an entity in scope for the EU NIS2 Directive (Network and Information Security Directive 2), Helios must protect the confidentiality, integrity, and availability of two estates: a modern corporate IT environment carrying customer and billing data, and a legacy, safety-critical OT environment (Operational Technology — the SCADA and substation control systems that keep power flowing). Acting now matters because the smart-grid rollout is networking systems that were historically isolated, a neighboring European utility has already suffered an OT-disrupting attack, and formal BSI (Bundesamt für Sicherheit in der Informationstechnik) audit engagement is approaching under compressed statutory timelines.

The program establishes one governance authority — the IT/OT Security Governance Council, chaired by the CISO (Chief Information Security Officer) — and organizes all remediation under the six NIST CSF 2.0 (Cybersecurity Framework) functions. **Govern** sets a single risk-appetite statement and closes the NIS2 Article 20 accountability gap. 
**Identify** builds one asset inventory and risk register spanning IT and OT. 
**Protect** delivers centralized identity and access management, network segmentation, and data protection. 
**Detect** extends SOC (Security Operations Center) monitoring into OT telemetry. **Respond** and **Recover** establish a joint IT/OT incident and crisis-management capability tested against NIS2's 24-hour, 72-hour, and one-month reporting clock.

Weighted across the six CSF functions — Protect and Identify weighted highest given OT's safety and reliability consequences — Helios's current program maturity is **1.2 out of 4 (30%) as of the 13 July 2026 audit date**. This is not an estimate of effort or intent; it reflects two Critical and two High findings in the regulatory audit and a ten-domain gap assessment showing no unified governance structure, no consolidated asset inventory, and IT/OT security still run as two disconnected functions.

Three gaps should anchor the first funding cycle. 
**Network segmentation** — OT is de facto flat-connected to IT with no defined zones or security-level targets — sits with OT Engineering and Network Security at an estimated €300K–€600K; left open, a single IT compromise has a direct path to safety-critical substation control, the same pattern seen at the neighboring utility. 
**Identity and access management** — vendor remote-access paths into OT are poorly inventoried, inconsistently multi-factor-protected, and in some cases still active on lapsed contracts — sits with IAM/Security at €200K–€400K; it is currently the highest-likelihood initial-access vector into the control environment. 
**OT vulnerability and patch management** — unsupported SCADA components with no documented compensating controls — sits with OT Engineering and Security at €150K–€350K; every quarter it remains open is a quarter of exploitable exposure with no risk-acceptance record an auditor could rely on.

**The decision requested of the Board:** approve release of €650K–€1.35M in initial-phase funding to close these three gaps and formally charter the IT/OT Security Governance Council, so remediation is underway — and evidenced — before the next BSI/KRITIS audit cycle.
