# Helios-Grid-Utilities-GRC-Program


# Executive Summary

Helios Grid Utilities is a regional electricity distribution operator serving approximately 1.4 million customers across Frankfurt and surrounding districts. As an essential entity under the EU NIS2 Directive and Germany's KRITIS/BSI regime, the organization must protect both its corporate IT environment and its operational technology (OT) that supports electricity distribution. Recent audit evidence shows that the convergence of legacy SCADA systems, smart-grid modernization, and fragmented IT/OT governance has increased operational, regulatory, and public-safety risk, making a unified cybersecurity program an immediate business priority.

This cybersecurity program consolidates governance, risk management, compliance, audit, asset management, identity and access management, security awareness, data protection, incident response, and third-party risk into a single framework aligned with the six NIST Cybersecurity Framework (CSF) 2.0 functions: Govern (governance structure, policies, third-party oversight), Identify (enterprise asset inventory, risk register, gap assessment), Protect (IAM, awareness, and DLP controls), Detect (audit findings, monitoring, and control testing), Respond (integrated IT/OT incident response and regulatory reporting), and Recover (resilience and restoration planning). Together these artifacts provide traceable evidence supporting NIS2, IEC 62443, ISO/IEC 27001, and BSI/KRITIS compliance.

Based on the assembled program artifacts, Helios's current cybersecurity maturity is assessed at 2.7/5 (Developing) as of 29 July 2026. Governance documentation, policies, and risk management processes have now been established; however, implementation maturity remains limited. Evidence includes the regulatory audit identifying two Critical and two High findings, the gap assessment highlighting critical weaknesses in network segmentation, identity and access management, and OT vulnerability management, and the enterprise risk register recording multiple risks with inherent scores of 20 (Critical) before treatment.

The three highest-priority remediation initiatives are: 
(1) Establish a unified IT/OT asset inventory and governance model (Owner: CISO and GRC Function; Cost: Medium; Business consequence if delayed: incomplete visibility, ineffective risk management, and inability to demonstrate regulatory compliance). 

(2) Secure OT identity and third-party remote access through centralized IAM, privileged access management, and mandatory multifactor authentication (Owner: IAM Lead and OT Security Lead; Cost: Medium–High; Business consequence: increased likelihood of unauthorized access leading to grid disruption and regulatory enforcement).

(3) Implement risk-based OT segmentation, monitoring, and vulnerability management aligned with IEC 62443 (Owner: OT Security Lead; Cost: High; Business consequence: elevated risk of operational outages, public-safety impacts, and significant financial penalties following a cyber incident).

Executive Decision Requested: Approve the Unified IT/OT Cybersecurity Governance Program, endorse the proposed remediation roadmap, assign executive accountability through the IT/OT Security Governance Council, and fund the priority initiatives required to achieve demonstrable NIS2 and KRITIS compliance while reducing cyber risk to critical grid operations.
